The rules it's built on

Unknown does not mean safe.

If WardenOne doesn't know, it says it doesn't know. A file with nothing strange in it isn't called harmless, and a search result never gets a green tick just because nothing matched.

  1. 01

    Nothing found means nothing found.

    When WardenOne finds nothing, it says it found nothing. It won't tell you a page is “safe”.

  2. 02

    Failures stay visible.

    A rule it couldn't apply, a missing part, a verdict it isn't sure about: you get to see it. Nothing is quietly ignored.

  3. 03

    Back off a little at a time.

    If a protection breaks a site, pause that site, turn off one protection there, or undo one rule. Never everything at once.

Privacy, plainly

No account. No telemetry. No WardenOne server.

Your browsing never passes through a server of mine, because there isn't one. A few requests do leave the browser, and every one of them is named.

{{icon:database}}

Stays in your browser

Your settings, exceptions and a bounded activity record: at most 200 events, none older than 30 days. Clear it whenever you like.

A note from the person who made it

I made WardenOne because I was tired of security tools that just ask you to trust them. A counter ticks up somewhere, and you never actually find out what happened or why.

I'm a recent software engineering graduate who's a little bit obsessed with security, and I know a new developer with a handful of followers is hard to trust. So I'm not asking you to. All of it is open source, it shows you why it stepped in, and when WardenOne isn't sure about something, it tells you.

Your browser should be looking out for you, not quietly working against you. 💜
