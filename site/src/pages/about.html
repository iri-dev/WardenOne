About

Made by one person, in the open.

WardenOne is an independent, open-source project by iri. It brings browser protection, practical controls and local visibility together in one extension, and it's built to explain itself.

{{icon:user}}It's just meThere's no security company behind WardenOne and it hasn't been certified by anyone. It misses things sometimes, too. The code and its known limits are public, so you can look for yourself and take your time deciding whether to trust it.

Why it exists

Feeling safe online shouldn't take five extensions.

If you care about privacy and security, you probably know the routine. An ad blocker, then a tracker blocker, then something for scams, maybe a download checker if you're being careful. Each one does its own little job, none of them talk to each other, and none of them really explain themselves.

I wanted one extension that does the whole job properly and shows its working. When it steps in, you can see what it did and why. When it isn't sure, it says so instead of guessing.

And it shouldn't cost you anything, not money and not your data. There's no account, no tracking, and nothing gets sent back to me.

The name

Why “WardenOne”?

{{icon:shield}}

Warden is the job

A good warden keeps watch quietly and only steps in when something is genuinely wrong. That's the goal: stay out of your way, act on real evidence, and never claim to know more than it does.

{{icon:hub}}

One is how it's built

Every part of WardenOne reports to the same place. A shady redirect can inform a scam warning, a download can borrow what's known about the page it came from, and when a site breaks you can follow one record straight back to the decision that caused it.

It doesn't mean one extension can stop everything bad on the internet. Nothing can, and I'd rather be honest about that than promise you magic.

How it's built

Built slowly, tested properly, fixed in the open.

WardenOne runs in my own browser while I build it, so I'm usually the first to find out when something breaks. A lot of my time goes into poking at one stubborn site until it behaves, then making sure the fix doesn't break three others.

Nothing ships until it gets through the project's gate: {{stat:testSuites}} automated test suites, plus checks that generated files still match the source they're built from. When I get something wrong, the fix goes up in public, mistakes and all.

What guides it

Inspect the implementation

The extension code, bundled rules, tests and documentation are all in the public repository, under the GNU GPL v3 or later.

{{icon:eye-off}}

Make data flows explicit

No WardenOne account and no telemetry. The privacy policy names every automatic request and every optional provider.

{{icon:globe}}

Respect the site you meant to use

Protection that breaks a legitimate page is a bug. It needs investigating, scoped rules and a way to recover.

Why the source matters

Someone once copied WardenOne to spread malware.

In August 2026 someone republished the project under another account and pointed its downloads at a credential stealer. GitHub took the account and site down. A security extension has a lot of access to your browser, so where you got it from really matters.

Releases only ever come from github.com/iri-dev/WardenOne, and WardenOne is never an installer.
