Redirects & popups

Block forced popups
Stops popunders and new tabs a page opens without a real user action. Catches timer-based window.open tricks, hidden ad tabs, and background popups that appear after the page loads. On by default.
Strict ad-popup shield
Blocks suspicious ad/affiliate tabs opened on click, including installer ads and "download + ad tab" tricks. Player controls, real downloads, and sign-ins stay native. On by default.
Block in-page popups & overlays
Removes fake notification bells, subscribe panels, adblock nags, cookie/continue walls, and download-gate popups that sit on top of videos or pages. Shows an Undo chip when it hides something. Turn off if a site hides real content.
Auto-skip download ads
On download-gate pages, silently follows the site's own "No thanks", "continue", or real download path so the ad layer disappears and the intended file can continue. Useful for fake "Download Now" ad funnels.
Block gestureless redirects
Blocks the page from sending you to a different site when there was no recent click, form submit, or other real gesture. Catches delayed ad hops, adult redirects, and fake-download redirects.
Block popup and redirect tricks
Takes away the tricks a page uses to get a click it can spend: fake "Please confirm to continue" prompts, script-built frames holding a bare INSTALL badge, and embedded players that move your whole tab when you click them. Real confirmations, payment forms, captchas and ordinary players are left alone.
Stop back-button traps
Three ways a page stops Back from working, all refused: putting the address you are already on straight back each time you press it; quietly stacking entries while you read, so Back has to be pressed once for every one of them; and shoving you forward again the instant you press Back. What separates a trap from an app you are using is whether anything you did asked for it, so ordinary browsing is untouched. Nothing already in your history is changed or removed — declining to add an entry is not the same as taking one away.
Detect redirect chains
Watches server-side redirect hops and freezes long or rotating CPA chains before the final landing page. Helps when the URL changes too quickly for normal in-page checks.
Block meta-refresh bounces
Stops pages from using <meta refresh> or refresh-style timers to move you off-site after a countdown. These are common on fake download and traffic-exchange pages.

IP protection

Block IP-grabber beacons
Someone can hide a logger in an ordinary-looking page — an image or a background request to a grabber service — and your address is recorded just by the page loading. This stops those requests before they leave, so there is nothing to record.
Warn on IP-logger pages
The other half: a link sent to you that goes to the grabber itself — Grabify, IPLogger and the rest — usually dressed up as a redirect to somewhere you meant to go. You get told what the page is before you use it. The page still loads; nothing is blocked here.
IP privacy guard
Hide WebRTC IP leaks from page scripts and block common third-party IP lookup APIs. A VPN or proxy is still needed to hide your IP from sites you directly visit.
Force HTTPS
Automatically upgrade insecure http:// pages to secure https:// before they load. Seamless on sites that support it.
Warn before an insecure sign-in
Stops you the moment you click into a password box on an unencrypted page, before you've typed anything, and offers the secure version of the site. Also catches the sneakier case: a page showing a padlock whose form still posts over plain http. Router and other local-network logins are left alone.
Block bad certificates
Show a WardenOne trust block when Chrome reports expired, revoked, self-signed, hostname-mismatch, or broken TLS certificates.

Download Shield

Check download domain age
Opt-in domain-age lookup for suspicious downloads. Uses RDAP by default, or WhoisXML enrichment when enabled. Sends only the source domain, never the file or full URL.

Privacy

Block trackers & ad analytics
Hard-block third-party tracking networks (Google Analytics, DoubleClick, Facebook Pixel, etc.)
Do Not Track & privacy signals
Tell sites you opt out of tracking via Do-Not-Track and Global Privacy Control (GPC has legal weight under CCPA)
Reduce third-party Client Hints
Removes detailed browser version, OS version, architecture, bitness, and device model from third-party requests. Known tracker hosts also lose basic browser hints. First-party sites keep their normal compatibility data; Anti-fingerprinting mode reduces the matching navigator.userAgentData values too.
Anti-fingerprinting noise
Optional randomization for canvas, WebGL vendor strings, audio buffers, and coarse hardware hints. It can break canvas-heavy sites and may make rare setups more unique, so it stays opt-in.
Fingerprint probe detection
Detect canvas, audio, WebGL, font, layout, and device probing without changing values. Feeds the local risk detector and tracker learner.
Block known fingerprinting scripts
Blocks a third-party-only list of known fingerprinting vendors before random spoofing. Keeps compatibility higher.
Block third-party cookies
Strips Set-Cookie from third-party pixels and beacons so they cannot store new cross-site tracking cookies, and clears cookies and storage inside frames belonging to known trackers. It is deliberately not applied to frames, scripts and XHR in general: sign-in and federation set their cookies on exactly those, and stripping them signs you out. Cookies a site already saved can still be sent. First-party logins keep working.
Login compatibility
Keeps anti-tracking on, but exempts official sign-in, CAPTCHA, and payment endpoints so Google/Microsoft/Apple/Okta/Auth0/PayPal/Stripe-style logins can finish. On by default.
No persistent cookies
Wipes every cookie when you fully close the browser, so nothing tracks you across sessions. Logins still work while you browse (you may just re-sign-in next launch). For trackers, use Block third-party cookies. Opt-in.
Block first-party trackers
Catch analytics proxied through the site's own domain (the trick used to dodge blocklists). Opt-in.
Local tracker learner
Learn third-party trackers locally after they appear across multiple sites. No browsing profile leaves this device.
Block tracker cache validators
Removes If-None-Match and ETag only on passive requests to WardenOne's known tracker hosts, preventing cache-tag respawning without disabling normal browser caching. Opt-in because repeated tracker responses can use more bandwidth.
De-AMP pages
Skip Google/AMP cache pages and go straight to the real article. Rewrites AMP links and redirects AMP pages to their original URL. Opt-in.
Strict cross-site referrer protection
Removes the Referer header on cross-site requests while keeping same-site referrers. Sign-in, CAPTCHA, and payment destinations are excluded for compatibility. Opt-in because some hotlink and hand-off flows rely on a referrer.
Auto-reject cookie banners
When the banner offers a way to say no, WardenOne takes it — opening the choices if it has to, turning off optional tracking, never clicking Accept. On by default.
Lift full-screen cookie walls
When it offers none: the sheet that covers the page and freezes scrolling. Lifted without clicking anything, so nothing is consented to. Goes back if the page turns out to be empty behind it. Some sites keep the wall on a separate domain, where nothing can reach it. Opt-in.
Clear a site's cookies after accepting
When you accept one yourself. The site's consent and tracking cookies, and the IDs it stored, are cleared once you leave. Sign-ins are left alone. Off by default.
Remove a site's service worker when you leave
A service worker is the one thing a site leaves running after you close the tab — it sits in front of every later request to that site and can wake up on its own. This removes it once you have closed every tab for a site that installed one while you were there. Off by default, because sites that legitimately use one for offline reading or notifications will lose that until you visit again.
Clean tracking redirects on links
Rewrite outbound links that hide the real destination behind a tracker (Facebook l.php, Google /url, Reddit out, etc.) so a click goes straight there.
Clean links copied inside pages
Auto-strips tracking (utm_, fbclid, YouTube si, Amazon ref and similar) as you copy, so pastes come out clean. Covers Ctrl+C on a link and site Share/Copy buttons. Chrome’s own “Copy link address” and the address bar are browser-level, so no extension can catch them — for those use the right-click “Copy clean link”, or Alt+Shift+C for this page. On by default.
Click to load social embeds
Replace Facebook, X, Instagram and TikTok embeds with a placeholder so they can't load or track you until you click. Opt-in.
Clear tracker supercookies
Wipe localStorage in known third-party tracker frames (Scorecard, Criteo, Taboola, etc.) so they can't re-identify you after a cookie clear.

Twitch local rewind▾
Watch from earlier (stream rewind)
Adds a "Rewind" button by the player controls that lets you jump back to before you opened the tab — from when you joined, 30 minutes back, or the very start — and watch what you missed in Twitch's normal player. Works only when the streamer saves past broadcasts (VODs); the button appears only on streams where it's available. No recording, no extra memory. On by default.
Twitch local rewind (DVR)
Adds 10s / 30s / 1m / 5m rewind buttons and a scrub timeline beside Twitch's volume control, plus speed (1–2×, eases back to live) and keyboard control (←/→ seek 10s, ,/. frame-step). Delayed playback keeps running until you press LIVE. It records the live stream into your computer's memory while the tab is open, so it uses noticeably more RAM and CPU (and more with a longer buffer or higher stream quality). It cannot recover video from before the tab started watching. Opt-in.
Buffer length (minutes)
How far back you can rewind. A longer buffer uses more RAM and CPU. Default 5; range 1–30.

Media Shield

Media Shield
Watch camera, microphone, screen-share, hidden media, and autoplay abuse
Full-screen address bar
In full screen the browser's own address bar is hidden, so a page can paint a fake one showing a site it isn't. Warns when a page does that — video, games and slideshows are untouched.
Fake sign-in windows
Warns when a page draws a box made to look like a separate browser sign-in window — title bar, close button, an address bar it painted itself — and asks for a password in it. Online IDEs and design tools are excluded.
Notification bait
Flags pages that talk you into clicking Allow on the notification prompt, and alerts that arrive reading like “your PC is infected”. Nothing is suppressed — alerts pushed from a service worker are outside a page script's reach.
Block camera & microphone
Stops sites opening camera or microphone prompts, and blocks capture started without you doing anything at all. That includes speech recognition, which reaches the microphone by a different route and would otherwise listen with nothing here noticing — and which Chrome sends away to be transcribed rather than doing on your machine. YouTube, Twitch and X are exempt so calls and uploads still work; anywhere else, a site that wants the microphone has to ask when you are actually there.
Block screen capture
Stops a page asking to record your screen or a window. On by default because a screen-share prompt you did not go looking for is almost always a scam walking you into one. YouTube, Twitch and X are exempt so ordinary players and calls still work.
Block location requests
Deny browser location prompts, kill location watches, mask page timezone/language hints, and ask Chrome to block location access globally. A VPN is still needed to hide IP-based location.
Block hidden media abuse
Stops players that run with nothing on screen — the ones that exist to autoplay an ad, hold a wake-lock, or keep a tab alive. Visible players you can see and pause are left alone, as are YouTube, Twitch and X.

Adult-site safety

Warn on adult-site redirects
Shows an "are you sure?" screen when you arrive on an 18+ site you did not go looking for, so a mistyped address or a sneaky redirect never drops you (or a kid on the family computer) straight onto explicit content. Going there deliberately still works.
Catch unlisted adult sites
Catches adult sites that are not on any list yet, by how the page behaves rather than by name. Newly registered sites rotate faster than any list can follow. It judges on signals, so it will occasionally be wrong — the gate is a question, never a block.
Force SafeSearch
Locks the Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Brave Search and Yahoo search engines into SafeSearch, and YouTube into Restricted Mode. The adult-site guard only fires when you arrive somewhere — explicit images and video render inside the results page itself, where there's no arrival to catch. This closes that gap. Off by default: it changes what search will show you.

Advanced detection (catches rotating/custom domains)

Warn on redirecting links
Flags links that carry another address inside them — the ?url= and ?redirect= shape — so a link that looks like it stays on this site but hands you to another one says so first.
Warn on shortened links
Flags bit.ly, tinyurl and the rest, which hide where a link actually goes until you are already there. You get told what it is before you follow it; the link still works if you want it.
Monitor for tracker requests
Warns when a page quietly posts to a logging endpoint on someone else's domain. Hosts the network rules already block stay silent, so this only speaks about the ones that would otherwise have got through unseen.
Detect fake / phishing sites
Warns when a domain is dressed up as one you know — a swapped letter, a lookalike character, the right brand on the wrong suffix. It reads the address rather than a list, so it catches ones registered this morning.
Login page age check
When a site asks for a password, checks how recently its domain was registered. A brand-new domain with a login form is a strong phishing signal, so it warns before you sign in. Free, no API key. Off by default because it is the one feature that leaves your device: it sends that site's domain (nothing else — no URL, no page content) to the public RDAP service at rdap.org, which would not otherwise see your browsing. Answers are cached on your device.
Block high-confidence look-alikes
For the clearest fakes (visual look-alikes like g00gle, keyboard typos, brand-on-wrong-TLD, homographs), show a full blocking screen instead of just a warning. Opt-in.
Behavioral risk detection
Catches brand-new / unknown sites that behave like trackers or scams — phoning home on arrival, random throwaway domains — even when they're on no blocklist. Warns; doesn't block.
XSS Behavior Guard
Warns when a value from the address bar, another site, or a page message reaches somewhere the browser would run it as code — the shape of a cross-site scripting attempt. Normal app routing, uploads and escaped examples are left alone, and the value itself is never recorded.
Script drift guard
Baselines third-party script hashes and warns when the same CDN/script URL later serves changed code with new behavior or outbound hosts. On by default.
Risky-site mode
New-looking or suspicious domains get stricter third-party scripts, frames, media, and background calls.
Anti-clickjacking checks
Warns before a click that matters — sign in, password, checkout, pay, transfer, authorise, allow, install — lands on something hidden, covered by another element, or otherwise not where it looks. Ordinary buttons in plain sight never raise it.
Intranet/router protection
Public pages cannot silently reach local admin panels — fetch, XHR, forms, beacons, sockets, scripts, frames and media are all covered. This works by reading the address a page asks for, so it stops anything aimed at your network by name or by IP. A public-looking name that quietly resolves to your router is a different problem, handled below.
DNS rebinding detection
Watches the address each site actually resolves to. When a normal-looking name comes back pointing at your own network — or answers publicly once and privately the next time, which is what a rebinding attack looks like — that name is blocked for the rest of the session. Being honest about the limit: the browser gives extensions no way to check an address before the request goes out, so the request that reveals the trick has already happened. This catches everything after it, not the first one. Pages you open yourself that live on your own network, like a local dev server, are left alone.
Enforce it at the network layer too
The protection above works by rewriting what a page can call. A background worker gets its own private copy of those same functions, untouched, so a few lines running in one could reach your network with the guard still sitting there. This adds a second rule that refuses local addresses no matter which part of a page asked, which is the only way to cover workers without rewriting their code and breaking them. Pages you opened from your own network keep full access to it, including other devices on it. Turn this off if something on your network stops loading and you would rather have it back.
Watch cross-site cookie requests
Something embedded in a page — a comment box, a video player, an ad frame — can ask for its own cookies back across sites, using the one route browsers still allow. You were never shown who asked. WardenOne records every request with the name of whoever made it, refuses the ones from known trackers outright, and flags anything that asks while invisible or without you clicking a thing. Ordinary embedded sign-ins are left to work.
Refuse every cross-site cookie request
Turns the above from watching into refusing: no embedded thing gets its cookies back across sites, tracker or not. Off by default, and left out of “Turn everything on”, because this is the mechanism behind signing in through an embedded box — comment logins, embedded checkouts, some SSO frames. Turn it on if you would rather those break than let anything through.

SessionShield — login & session protection

SessionShield
Watch for login/session tokens exposed in risky places (localStorage, URLs, etc.) and check cookie safety. Never stores or shows full tokens.
Protect session tokens
Block token-shaped values from leaving a site for another domain. Keeps protection on, but treats noisy trusted services more calmly in the activity log. Includes outgoing requests made inside embedded frames.
Continuous token watch
Detect tokens the moment they're written to storage (e.g. after login), not just at page load.
Detect & block form skimmers
Spots third-party scripts reading password/card fields on payment pages, and blocks card or password data being sent to an off-site address (Magecart-style theft). Includes forms and request APIs inside embedded frames.
Payment card guard
Warns before card details leave scammy, very new, suspicious, or reputation-flagged checkout pages, and blocks cards on insecure, look-alike, raw-IP, or known-dangerous payment forms. Embedded frames receive a lightweight card-exfiltration guard; full checkout and reputation analysis stays on the top page.
Clipboard hijack protection
Stop sites that secretly swap your clipboard — e.g. replacing a copied crypto wallet address with an attacker's. Keeps normal Copy buttons working. Opt-in.
Clipboard swap detection
Catches clipboard-swapping malware at paste time: if you paste a crypto address that differs from the one you just copied (same coin, within 2 minutes), it warns and shows both so you can check before sending. Works even against malware outside the browser. On by default.
Keystroke pressure detector
Warns when a page attaches an unusually large number of global keystroke listeners — the pattern a keylogger uses. It's a rough heuristic: rich editors and AI chat apps also attach many, so it can over-warn. Off by default; turn on if you want it.
Honeytoken mode
Plants fake decoy secrets (api-key, token...) in page memory that real site code never touches. If a script reads one, it's credential-recon behavior and you're warned. Experimental and safe (it yields if the page actually uses a name), but off by default.
Command paste guard
Detects ClickFix and self-XSS instructions such as Win+R, F12, Ctrl+Shift+I, DevTools, Console, PowerShell, and “enable pasting,” including fake “verify you’re human” steps. Suspicious programmatic clipboard writes are blocked, risky copied selections trigger a warning, and instruction-plus-command matches receive a stronger warning. WardenOne does not access Chrome’s own DevTools interface. On by default.
Tech-support scam guard
Detects fake "your computer is infected / locked — call this number" pages and browser-lockers (pop-up dialog spam). Neutralizes the lock, frees you to leave, and shows a calm warning that your PC is fine. On by default.
Paste protection
Warns before you paste a password, API token, private key, or seed phrase into a risky page — an insecure http:// page, a look-alike/phishing domain, or a form that submits to a different site. Stays silent on normal secure login forms. On by default.
Form trap detector
Inspects login forms and warns if one looks fake or credential-stealing — e.g. it posts your password to a different site or raw IP, claims to be a brand the site isn't, or is an injected overlay. Known sign-in providers (Google, Microsoft, Okta...) are trusted, so real logins stay silent. On by default.
Fake update detector
Warns on "your browser/Windows is out of date — update now" pages that aren't a real vendor (Google, Microsoft, Mozilla...). Real updates never come from a web page, so a fake-update lure plus an installer download or off-site button is a common malware scam. On by default.
OAuth grant guard
Warns on risky Google, Microsoft, GitHub, or Discord consent grants, like email, contacts, repositories, admin, offline access, or unsafe redirect combinations. On by default.
Breach & site-history checks
Allow the "has this site been breached?" lookup below. Contacts the public Have I Been Pwned database. Opt-in.

Blocklist (auto-updating)

Block known malicious sites
Hard-blocks known malware, phishing and scam domains from vetted threat feeds — tens of thousands of them, millions across the feeds — before the page loads at all. This is the one to leave on if you leave nothing else.
Block cryptominers
Stop drive-by mining scripts that spend your CPU and battery, and block pages from reaching mining pools. Mining pools stay reachable if you visit them yourself.
Deep cryptominer detection
Catches miners a site hosts itself, which blocking can't see: reads the code of the background workers a page starts, and stops the ones running mining routines — including the replacements a miner starts when you kill it. Only the mining worker is stopped; the rest of the site keeps working. Off by default because it reads page code on every site you visit. Allowlist a site to leave it alone.
Auto-update blocklist
Pulls fresh phishing, scam, IP-logger, adult-warning and page-rule lists once a day. The built-in rules still work without it, but new threats stop arriving — and a list that failed to fetch keeps the copy already downloaded rather than leaving a gap.
Blocklist: built-in
Updates automatically once a day

One rule per line. ||ads.example.com^ blocks requests to a host, @@||example.com^ stops blocking one, example.com##.promo hides an element on one site, and ##.promo hides it everywhere. Lines starting with ! are notes. Anything WardenOne cannot use is listed underneath rather than silently ignored.

EyeShield

Extras

What WardenOne watches

These three only ever write to the Activity Center. They block nothing and change nothing on the page, so there is no protection here to switch off — they are listed so you can see what is being watched. The master switch and the site allowlist still turn them off with everything else.
Background reports
Pages send measurement to other companies in the background now, where a tracking pixel used to be. Those go past on no blocklist and leave no trace, so the destination is noted here — the host, once per page. What was sent is never read or stored.
Hardware and file access
When a site asks to talk to a USB, serial, HID or Bluetooth device, or asks for a MIDI device or a file or folder on your computer — and separately when it comes back to one you granted on an earlier visit, which needs no prompt and so can happen while you are not looking. Folder access reaches furthest of any of these: a whole tree, and it persists, which is why "pick your Downloads folder so we can scan it" is worth recognising. Chrome's own picker still decides. The device, file and folder are never recorded — only which kind of access, and whether it was read or write.
Browser capabilities
Four things that leave no other trace. Installing a service worker is the one that outlasts the visit — it stays after the tab closes and sits in front of every later request to the site, which is how offline and push work and also how a page leaves something behind. Asking to install itself matters next: an installed site opens in its own window with no address bar — the same blind spot the fake-window and full-screen guards exist for. Idle detection tells a site when you are at your desk and when your screen is locked. Chrome's payment sheet is a route to card details the form guard cannot see. Chrome still confirms each one; only which payment methods were offered is noted, never the amount or the item.
